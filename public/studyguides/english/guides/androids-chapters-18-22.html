01 / Before Chapter 18

Start with the world and the people

A self-contained briefing for a first-time reader. Chapters 1–17 supply necessary context; Chapters 18–22 cover the novel’s ending. Follow who knows what, distinguish reports from thoughts, and connect actions with their immediate situations.

02 / Storyline

A refuge, a bargain, a test of care

Follow the two narrative strands from Isidore’s dinner to the Chapter 18 alarm. Select a node to read its causes and consequences; arrows show how the sequence develops.

Select a node to inspect its evidence

Reading distinction: A character’s assertion is evidence of that character’s position. It becomes a confirmed fact only when the chapter supplies sufficient verification.

03 / Detail recall

The person, the object, the exact record

Recall the answer before opening each card. Chapter and PDF locators follow the retained classroom edition. Keep who supplies an object distinct from who uses it, and a reported claim distinct from a confirmed result.

04 / Character close reading

Behavior. Tone. Thought.

Twenty scene-based readings, at least four per chapter. Read the situation and cue, follow the reasoning, then test the claim’s limits. Scene cues are paraphrases; verified short fragments appear in Original evidence.

05 / Themes & synthesis

Name the idea. Explain the evidence.

Seven thematic arguments connect the spider, final hunt, goat, solitary climb and electrical toad. Each includes a claim, concrete evidence and a limit on interpretation.

06 / Original evidence

The words and what they support

Short original fragments are followed by their speaker, situation, close reading, and limits. Chapter references work across editions; PDF page links locate the exact fragment in the linked English text.

Using a passage in a short answer

Identify the speaker and situation. Select a precise detail, then explain how its wording or placement supports your claim. Connect that explanation to the prompt. A longer quotation does not replace commentary; a paraphrased event can also be valid evidence.

07 / Mind map

Select a branch. Each interpretation includes a chapter event you can reopen in the storyline.

08 / Question types

Identify the task first

Detail questions ask what happened. Concept questions ask what a term means. Analysis questions ask how a choice produces an effect.

Sources, chapter references, and study route

Study route: Earlier context → story flow → detail recall → character close reading → named themes → original evidence → mind map → answer templates → self-test → predicted priorities.
